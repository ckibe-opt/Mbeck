# Mbeck Business Model: Revenue & Target Market Strategy

**Date:** February 1, 2026  
**Version:** 1.0.0 (Commercial Strategy)  
**Status:** Verification Phase

---

## 1. Target Audience Segmentation

The platform is designed to dominate the "Unorganized Retail" sector in emerging markets (Sub-Saharan Africa, SE Asia, LATAM).

### 1.1 The "Alpha" User: The High-Volume Kiosk
*   **Profile:** Fast-Moving Consumer Goods (FMCG) shop. Handles >200 transactions/day.
*   **Pain Point:** Speed. Scanning barcodes or typing prices manually is too slow for rush hour.
*   **Why Mbeck?** "Point and Click" is 5x faster. Offline reliability fits their potentially unstable internet.
*   **Customer Lifetime Value (CLTV):** High (Subscription potential).

### 1.2 The "Beta" User: The Boutique / Hardware Store
*   **Profile:** Specialized inventory (Clothes, Spare Parts).
*   **Pain Point:** Inventory Tracking. "Do I have this bolt in size 10mm?"
*   **Why Mbeck?** Visual search finds the item even if the staff doesn't know the name.
*   **CLTV:** Moderate (Value lies in data accuracy).

### 1.3 The "Gamma" User: The Informal Hawker
*   **Profile:** Temporary stall, selling 10-20 items.
*   **Pain Point:** Professionalism. Needs to send receipts to keep customers returning.
*   **Why Mbeck?** No hardware investment. Runs on their existing phone.
*   **CLTV:** Low (Likely Free Tier), but high viral coefficient.

---

## 2. Value Proposition (The "Switch" Incentive)

Why would a shop owner stop using a notebook or a legacy POS?

1.  **Zero Hardware Cost:** No \$400 terminal. No scanner gun. Just an Android phone.
2.  **Offline Independence:** "The system works even when Safaricom/Airtel is down." This is a killer feature for business continuity.
3.  **The "Cool" Factor:** Sending a digital receipt via WhatsApp/QR signals to customers that the shop is modern and trustworthy.
4.  **Theft Prevention:** Real-time stock tracking makes it harder for staff to pocket cash without recording sales.

---

## 3. Revenue Architecture

Mbeck employs a diversified revenue stack to avoid reliance on a single unstable stream.

### 3.1 SaaS Subscription (The Base)
*   **Freemium (Entry):** 50 Items, 1 Staff. *Goal: User Acquisition.*
*   **Pro (Grow):** \$10/month. Unlimited Items, 3 Staff, Cloud Backup, WhatsApp Receipts. *Goal: Sustainability.*
*   **Enterprise (Scale):** \$50/month per branch. HQ Analytics, API Access. *Goal: High Margin.*

### 3.2 Fintech & Payments (The Accelerator)
*   **Payment Processing:** Integration with M-Pesa/Mobile Money.
    *   *Take Rate:* 0.5% spread on top of carrier fees.
*   **Micro-Lending (High Yield):**
    *   Banks reject SMEs because they lack data.
    *   Mbeck has *verified sales data*.
    *   **Model:** Offer "Inventory Financing" (Buy stock now, pay later). Mbeck takes 2-5% interest/fee on the short-term loan, collateralized by the shop's future sales history.

### 3.3 Data Monetization (The Long Tail)
*   **FMCG Insights:** "Coca-Cola wants to know which flavor is selling best in Region X."
*   **Aggregated Reports:** Sell anonymized, granular consumption data to Brands, Distributors, and Market Researchers.

## 4. Acquisition Strategy (Go-to-Market)

### 4.1 The Viral Loop (The Receipt)
*   Every shared receipt is an ad.
*   **Mechanism:** When a Seller sends a WhatsApp receipt, the footer says "Powered by Mbeck - Download for Free."
*   **Target:** Customers of shops are often shop owners themselves.

### 4.2 The "Agent" Network
*   Leverage existing "Airtime Resellers" or ground agents.
*   **Commission:** Agent gets \$5 for every shop they onboard that pays for 1 month of Pro.
*   **Training:** Agents act as the "Technical Support" on the ground, helping users train their first 50 items.

## 5. Competitive Moat

| Competitor Type | Example | Weakness to Exploit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legacy POS** | Square, Toast | Requires expensive hardware & constant internet. |
| **Notebooks** | Pen & Paper | Data is dead. Cannot get loans. Frequent math errors. |
| **Telco Apps** | M-Pesa Business | Good for payments, terrible for Inventory Management. |

**Mbeck's Moat:** The **Visual Data**. Once a shop has trained 1,000 items with photos, switching costs are massive. They won't leave because no other system "knows" their inventory visually.
